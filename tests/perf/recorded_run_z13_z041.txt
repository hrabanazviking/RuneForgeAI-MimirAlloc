=== FINDINGS ===
1. For the banked mimir core, 16 pinned threads spread across both CCDs is the WORST pinned posture measured: 0.69-0.72x of the 8-thread one-CCD posture, identically in both workloads and both invocations (the tightest ratio in this slice). The remaining shared structures (central lock on cache miss / span supply, registry walks of Z-024) pay the Z-012 cross-CCD line price (~103 ns vs ~32 ns) as soon as the second CCD joins, and at 16 threads nothing compensates yet.
2. SMT is a real gain for allocator-heavy paths on this machine, for BOTH allocator classes: mimir pinned 32/16 = 1.46-1.88 (PROVEN), glibc 32/16 direction 4/4 in both modes. This does not contradict Z-011/Z-012: bandwidth-bound streaming gains nothing from siblings (Z-011: 32T 4-10% BELOW 16T) and a sibling is worth ~0.2 core of streaming (Z-012) - but the allocator path is latency/serialisation-bound, not bandwidth-bound, and siblings hide exactly that latency. SMT value is a property of the bottleneck, not of the core count.
3. Pinning never loses beyond a tie band and usually wins. At 8 threads it is worth 1.19-1.97x for mimir (scheduler placement of few floating threads is bimodal: floating t=8 small_churn reps split between ~16M and ~31M clusters inside one invocation; the probe does not distinguish which placement makes the slow cluster - sibling-packing and split-across-CCDs with shared-structure bouncing (finding 1) are both consistent with it - but pinned identity placement never produces the slow cluster). At 16-32 mimir threads pinning ties floating on medians (0.95-1.22) but removes the variance (pinned spreads <= 6.6% vs floating up to 93.7%). For glibc, pinned > floating in 12 of 12 pairs. Floating migration is wholesale: a median 26-30 of 32 floating workers end their pass on a different CPU than they started it.
4. Allocator class decides the thread-count answer (the disagreement is allocator-attributable, not hardware-attributable): the scalable glibc leg scales monotonically 8 -> 16 -> 32 in every pair, while the mimir leg dips at 16 (cross-CCD sharing) and recovers at 32 (SMT latency-hiding). A single machine-wide thread cap would be wrong for one of the two classes.
5. Best aggregate mimir posture measured: 32 pinned (33.2-33.8M small_churn, 39.7-39.9M mixed_batch ops/sec), edging 8 pinned one-CCD (1.05-1.09x small_churn, 1.28-1.29x mixed_batch) with far better per-thread cost at 8 (8 pinned per-thread ~3.9M ops/sec vs 32 pinned ~1.0M). Posture choice is aggregate-vs-efficiency, stated in the policy doc.
