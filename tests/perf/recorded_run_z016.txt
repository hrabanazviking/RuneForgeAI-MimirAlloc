method (Z-004 protocol: pinned, median of reps, load recorded):
  N threads each perform 2,000,000 relaxed atomic fetch_add increments
  on ONLY their own counter; nothing is logically shared. The sole
  variable is the counter layout (stride): same-line 8 B (8 counters
  share one 64 B line), neighbour 64 B (one counter per line, lines
  adjacent), padded128 (stride 128 B, one guard line), padded256
  (stride 256 B, three guard lines). 5 reps per cell, median reported.
  Work is verified every rep: sum of counters == threads * ITERS.
  Pinning fills physical cores first (Z-004 CCD law): thread order
  0-7 (CCD0), 8-15 (CCD1), 16-23 (CCD0 SMT), 24-31 (CCD1 SMT).
  So 2/4/8-thread rows are intra-CCD0; 16 threads span both CCDs on
  physical cores; 32 threads add every SMT sibling.
  Metric: aggregate ns/op = wall time / total increments across all
  threads. It FALLS as threads are added even for unshared layouts,
  because more threads complete more total work per wall second.
  The false-sharing signal is the RATIO between layouts at a FIXED
  thread count, never the raw ns/op trend across thread counts.
  Interpretation caveat for same-line rows above 8 threads: at
  stride 8 B the counters group 8 per line, so 16 threads contend
  on 2 lines x 8 writers and 32 threads on 4 lines x 8 writers.
  Per-line contention (8 writers) is constant across those rows;
  the aggregate ns/op falls because the line groups run in parallel.

=== FINDINGS ===
1. False sharing is the most expensive per-counter effect measured
   in Wave 2 so far. Packing 8 independently-written counters into
   one 64 B line costs 2.9x aggregate throughput at 2 threads,
   ~6x at 4 threads and ~11x at 8-16 threads versus giving each
   counter its own line. The penalty is a coherence-ownership
   effect, not a bandwidth effect: the shared line must change
   owner on nearly every increment.
2. One counter per 64 B line captures the ENTIRE win. Neighbour
   (stride 64, lines adjacent), padded128 and padded256 are within
   the 5% noise bound of each other at 8, 16 and 32 threads in
   both final runs: adjacent lines do not measurably interfere,
   and guard lines beyond line-per-counter buy nothing on this
   machine. Extra padding is pure footprint cost.
3. The same-line cost does not grow past 8 writers per line in
   aggregate terms (see the method caveat): per-line contention
   saturates at 8 writers, and extra thread groups on extra
   lines proceed in parallel. Design consequence: what matters
   is writers-per-line, not total thread count.

                          stat, queue head or flag per 64 B cache
                          line; no two hot writers share a line)

                          8-16 writers on one line (aggregate
                          throughput, this machine, 64 B lines)

                          noise of 64 B everywhere it matters;
                          pad to the line, not beyond it)

                          written by more than one thread gets its
                          own 64 B line (align + pad to 64 B);
                          read-mostly and single-writer fields are
