=== VERDICT - THE LARGE-PATH COST SPLIT (roadmap gate) ===
Gate status: MET. Cost split percentages are recorded below for the large_batch shape and per request size; KL-C's wound is sized in ns/op and as a ratio, per size and for the batch.

THE BATCH (large_batch as the roadmap defines it, 32 live, forward free):
  mimir total 2880.62 ns/op. Split: mmap/munmap pair 936.72 (32.5%), faults 1441.16 (50.0%), bookkeeping-residual 502.74 (17.5%).
  KL-C WOUND (batch): 2880.62 - 693.17 = 2187.45 ns/op, ratio 4.16x vs glibc in this run's window (Z-003 recorded 3064.69 vs 1295.68 = 2.4x under its own heavier contention; the wound's ns size is of the same order in both runs, the ratio moves mainly with the load-sensitive glibc leg - see the load note: post-run spot checks put glibc batch anywhere in 693-2141 ns/op while mimir held 2881-2939, so the honest statement is: wound about 2.2 us/op, ratio at least 4.2x in-window and larger when the glibc leg is calm).

PER SIZE (single block live; shares of the mimir total; full table in the summariser output):
  mean across the six sizes: pair 39.0%, faults 41.4%, bookkeeping-residual 19.6% of a 2713.34 ns/op mean total.
  The pair share FALLS with size (49.6% at 2048 -> 31.2% at 1048576) because the pair is size-flat (~1019-1157 ns at every size, one mmap + one munmap, untouched-page teardown has no per-page term - cross-checks Z-015) while the fault share grows: at 2048 the single user page faults once (1.000 fault/op counted); at every larger size the endpoint touch faults exactly 2 user pages (2.000/op counted) and the populated-teardown premium inside munmap grows with mapping length.
  Bookkeeping-residual is 402-810 ns/op (15.3-24.7%) at every size - size-flat, as the source says it must be (fixed descriptor write, O(1) insert/remove, fixed counters; the only count-dependent term is the free-side walk, sized separately below).

WHERE THE FAULTS GO (counted, not inferred): batch mimir faults 2.834 minor faults/op; batchraw touch faults 1.834/op. The difference is exactly +1.000 fault/op: the RESERVE PAGE. alloc_locked writes the 96-byte descriptor and the owner-thread record into the reserve page at publication, so every large allocation faults its reserve page once, on top of the 1-2 user pages the bench touch faults (per batch: rotations fault 58-59 user pages of ~1718-2035 mapped user pages, about 3.4% - the workload's interior pages are never touched). The reserve-page fault rides inside the bookkeeping residual in the split above (it is publication work), and is named here so the residual is not misread as pure CPU bookkeeping: roughly one Z-014 fault (345-505 ns/page quiet) of the 503 ns batch residual is that fault under this window's load. Cross-check vs constants: Z-014's quiet fresh-fault cost is 344-373 ns/page at these sizes; this run's faults delta per counted user fault is 622-722 ns - the delta is fault handling PLUS populated teardown (Z-015: populated munmap ~63-128 ns/page, and a just-faulted region's teardown under load), never fault handling alone; the two runs agree once the teardown term is admitted.

THE REGISTRY WALK (free-side bookkeeping that scales with live count): forward free is the worst geometry by construction - registry_insert is head-insertion and find_candidate_locked walks from the head, so freeing a batch oldest-first costs 32+31+...+1 = 528 pointer comparisons per batch (mean 16.5 per free; a reverse-order free would cost 1 per free). Timed free-all passes at 65536-byte blocks: mimir 1316.40 ns/free at 1 live, 1555.09 at 32, 1870.33 at 256, 4040.17 at 1024 (all drift-clean); raw munmap at the same live counts is flat (~1442-1503 in-window, drift-flagged, load-sensitive - indicative only). The mimir-side growth is the walk plus validation: about +2.7 us/free between 1 and 1024 live, i.e. the walk costs roughly 2-3 ns per live mapping per free on this host under this load, and it is paid on EVERY large free, under the global lock (Z-019: at saturation every cycle of hold is paid once per queued thread as wait). At large_batch's 32-live depth the walk is a minor term (~80 ns/free of excess in-window); at KL-D-style depths (hundreds live) it becomes a first-order term - that is a Z-024/Z-027 input, recorded here as measured.

GLIBC COMPARATOR ANATOMY (why the wound is structural, proven on this host): in a fresh process, glibc 2.44 serves 2048-65536 from the arena (mallinfo2 hblks stays 0; 65536 grows the arena by sbrk) and only 262144 and 1048576 take their own mmap (hblks +1 each) - the mmap threshold is exactly 128 KiB (131072 mmaps). After an mmapped chunk is freed the DYNAMIC threshold rises to that chunk's size (after freeing 1048576, even 1048576 itself is arena-served for the rest of the process). So in this probe's steady-state single-size loops glibc costs 8.31-8.87 ns/op at EVERY size including 1048576 - pure arena reuse, no syscall, no fault - while MimirAlloc pays a fresh mapping per allocation at every size past 1024. The per-size wound table (2278-3264 ns/op, ratios 260-394x) is therefore against glibc's steady state and is labelled as such; the batch wound (4.16x in-window) is the conservative headline because the batch's mixed sizes and 32-live shape partially defeat glibc's caching. KL-C in one sentence: MimirAlloc's large path spends about 2.9 us to do with a fresh mapping what glibc's threshold-and-cache wisdom usually does from memory it already holds - and of that 2.9 us, only about a fifth is MimirAlloc's own bookkeeping; the rest is the kernel mapping work (pair + faults) that a mapping-per-allocation design cannot avoid paying per op.

DENSE-TOUCH PRICING (auxiliary, not the bench): if a workload actually writes every user page, faults stop being sparse: raw/fulltouch costs 1865.64 ns at 2048 (1 page, same as touch) rising to 112928.90 ns at 1048576 (256 pages, ~441 ns/page in-window, cross-checks Z-014). A full-touch 1 MiB op would cost MimirAlloc roughly 113 us of faults alone - 35x its sparse-touch total - and would pay it AGAIN on every re-allocation, where a caching allocator re-faults nothing. The sparse bench understates KL-C for dense real workloads; the direction of the error is recorded, with its size.

DESIGN CONSEQUENCES (inputs to Z-026, no decision taken here): (1) any large-path cache/pool attacks 100% of the pair+fault shares (82.5% of the batch cost) - Z-014 already proved pooling buys no FAULT savings on recycled mappings, so a pool's win would come from avoiding the pair and the reserve-page fault, not from cheaper faults; (2) MADV_HUGEPAGE on pooled mappings at 2 MiB+ (Z-013: faults -89%) is the one lever that shrinks the fault share itself, and the constants doc's 200 MiB pooled-THP row is still OPEN; (3) the walk is a separate, smaller wound with a separate cure (an O(1) owner lookup or a pointer-keyed index), worth ~2-3 ns per live mapping per free; (4) bookkeeping CPU proper (lock, descriptor, counters) is the smallest share - optimising it first would chase the wrong fifth of the cost.

Bugs found/fixed in this slice's own work: (a) probe compile faults (feature-macro collision with the included bench, a now_ns name collision, MAP_ANONYMOUS visibility) caught at first compile and fixed in the probe before any run; (b) a batchraw seed-offset fault (timed batches numbered 30..329 instead of the bench's 0..299) caught by the checksum discipline - batchraw/touch read 1162896 against the K-002 1106008 - fixed and re-proven (1106008) before the recorded run. No allocator (production) bug was discovered by this slice; no production file is changed. One documentation flag from the team's cross-check, recorded for the keeper (outside this slice's artifacts): the Z-013 recorded run's load note claims a quiet window (1-min load <= 0.4) while its own embedded host-truth header records loadavg 4.87 - the Z-013 numbers are corroborated by Z-014/Z-015 and stand, but its load LABEL is wrong; docs/Z13_DESIGN_CONSTANTS.md s.5 repeats the label.
