DEFECTS FOUND BY THE STORM (both fixed in this slice, in
src/core/mimir_core.c; the storm failed until they were):
D1 - span released from under live bin-published blocks.
  small_free_locked's retention-release trigger required
  only live_count == 0 plus other empty spans over the
  bound; it did not require THIS span's binned tally to be
  0, unlike the retention sweep and the trim path, which
  both carry that guard. The tally also counts blocks a
  bin has published whose live transition is still pending
  in the allocating thread, so a span with live,
  not-yet-accounted blocks could be unlinked and unmapped;
  their later cross frees found an owner record but no
  registered span and silently did nothing, and a bin
  drain touching the unmapped span crashed one early run.
  Fix: the trigger now also requires
  tls_binned_count_locked(span) == 0.
D2 - pending-accounting wrap refuses live spans on the
  free path. A bin-hit publication's +1 is pending in the
  allocating thread until its next flush; a cross-thread
  free applies its -1 immediately (small_free_locked), so
  live_count transiently wraps (measured live_count =
  2^64-1, carve_count = 62, class 64B) and the J-006
  relation live_count <= carve_count in
  span_header_is_live() refused the span: instrumented
  runs showed 41-92 of ~391k cross frees taking the
  owner-remote route, finding the span in the registry,
  failing only that one relation, and silently leaking
  the block (shortfall == live excess in every case).
  Fix: the free path (small_free_locked and the two
  mimir_core_free pre-checks) now uses
  find_span_for_free_locked / span_header_is_live_for_free:
  every J-006 relation except the live_count bound -
  ownership of the exact block is still proven by the
  containment, stride, carve and free-list checks, and
  the counter arithmetic is exact in the mod-2^64 ring
  once every transition lands exactly once. Allocation-
  side callers keep the strict form.
  Named residual (not exercised by this storm): the
  realloc retire lookup at the old-block span check keeps
  the strict finder; in the same transient window it
  returns NULL with the original block preserved - a
  visible, retryable failure, not a silent leak. Recorded
  for the seat / Z-045 verdict.
Regression evidence post-fix: storm suite 42/42 on three

VERDICTS
- Roadmap gate: storm GREEN (42/42, both red paths fail
  as designed); worst-case remote-free latency RECORDED:
  1,074,081,376 ns (~1.07 s, attempt 2 per-process max
  median; per-process maxima across attempts 0.86-1.25 s)
  for a single cross-thread mimir_free in the 32-thread
  ring, against a median of 8.1-9.2 us and p99 of 35-72 us.
  The tail is the one-global-lock convoy of the banked
  core (Z-024 anatomy) at storm scale, amplified by
  descheduling inside the timed call; it is reported as
  measured, not explained away.
- Cross-free vs local on this core: ring32 wall is 2.9x
  the local32 control. Against glibc the direct remote
  free LOSES at every percentile (median ~300x glibc's
  30 ns tcache free; worst case ~4 orders). Proven loss,
  banked as such.
- Queue under flood (pre-registered): bypass 95.1-95.4%
  confirms the saturation regime. Freer-side tail: queue
  enqueue per-process max ~20 us vs direct free max ~1 s
  -> freer-side tail WIN, by ~45,000x. End-to-end tail:
  queue e2e max_med 1.3-1.8 ms also beats the direct
  max (owner batch-drain amortises the lock); the queue
  flood's system wall per offered block (62-78 ns,
  labelled unstable) is a different geometry from the
  32-way ring and is NOT a like-for-like allocator win.
  The Z-030 pair-geometry total-cost LOSS (+35.9%/+60.9%)
  stands uncontradicted: the queue remains test-only
  harness (NOT wired into mimir_free); production remote
  frees still take the locked central path ring32 measures.
- Standing gate: scripts/z13/run_gate.sh GREEN,
  4988 checks / 0 failed / 77 by-design skips
  (hidden_files/z043_gate.log), baseline held exactly,
  with both defect fixes in the tree.
