=== VERDICT - THE THRESHOLD STUDY (roadmap gate) ===
Gate status: MET. The per-decade cost table is filed below (and in the summariser output verbatim), and the campaign's cutoff options are priced under two reference mixes with a stated proxy and a sensitivity row.

PART 1 - GLIBC'S DYNAMIC MMAP THRESHOLD, MEASURED ON THIS HOST (glibc 2.44)
Virgin onset, exact to the byte: in a fresh process, requests up to 131048 bytes are arena-served (at 131048 the arena chunk is 131056 bytes: uordblks during = 131056) and requests from 131049 bytes mmap (hblks 0->1, hblkhd 135168). The threshold is on CHUNK size: chunk = round_up_16(request + 8), and a chunk of exactly 131072 = the 128 KiB default threshold mmaps. The often-quoted "128 KiB" is a chunk-size statement; in request terms the cliff on this host is 131048|131049. The mmap chunk's mapped length is chunk page-rounded plus one page (131072 request -> hblkhd 135168; 1048576 -> 1052672; 33554432 -> 33558528) - glibc's mmap geometry carries the same one-reserve-page shape as MimirAlloc's large path.
The dynamic rise: freeing an mmapped chunk raises the threshold to that chunk's size. Proven pair by pair (TD rows): after freeing 262144, probes 131072 and 262144 are ARENA and 524288 still MMAPs; after freeing 1048576, probes 524288 and 1048576 are ARENA and 2097152 MMAPs; after freeing 8388608, probes 4194304 and 8388608 are ARENA and 16777216 MMAPs. The rise does not decay: after the 1048576 rise, 2000 intervening 4096-byte alloc/free cycles leave the 524288 probe ARENA (TS row).
The cap is a CLIFF, not a clamp - the study's sharpest threshold finding: the rise happens only when the freed chunk is <= 33554432 bytes (32 MiB, = 4*1024*1024*sizeof(long) on 64-bit). Freeing a request of 33554400 (chunk 33554416, inside the cap) raises the threshold to 33554416: probe 16777216 ARENA, probe 33554400 MMAP (its chunk equals the raised threshold's next step), probe 33554432 MMAP. Freeing a request of 33554432 - whose chunk is 33554448, SIXTEEN BYTES over the cap - raises nothing at all: probe 16777216 still MMAPs. Freeing 67108864 likewise raises nothing (probe 16777216 MMAP). A process cannot earn dynamic shelter for the top decade by freeing inside it; shelter at the top must come from below the cap.
mallopt control: an explicit mallopt(M_MMAP_THRESHOLD, 131072) disables the dynamic adjustment exactly as documented - after freeing 8388608 the 4194304 probe still MMAPs (TM row 1); and an explicit mallopt(M_MMAP_THRESHOLD, 67108864) is honoured literally - the very first 1048576 allocation is already ARENA (TM row 2). The glmap cost leg (threshold pinned at 128) rests on the same disablement.
Consequence for every glibc comparison on this line (Z-023 already met it, now byte-exact): a long-running glibc process serves EVERY request from 131049 up to a 33554416 chunk from reused arena memory at ~8 ns/op once it has freed one such chunk - except requests whose chunk exceeds 33554432, which mmap forever. That exception is visible in the cost table itself: op/glibc is 7.90-9.28 ns/op at every ladder size except 33554432, where it jumps to 3282.89 - glibc paying the mapping path there too.

PART 2 - WHAT MIMIRALLOC'S FIXED 1024-BYTE CUTOFF COSTS, PER DECADE
The cliff at the cutoff, measured at the byte: op/mimir at 1024 (span path, TLS cache live - the seat core carries the banked Z-029 fix) is 23.98 ns/op; at 1025 (mapping path) a 3-process boundary exhibit read 1961.84 / 1938.55 / 1902.17 ns/op (median ~1939); at 2048 the ladder median is 1911.00. ONE BYTE past the cutoff multiplies the price about 80x. No other boundary in either allocator is this steep: glibc's own cliff at 131048|131049 is invisible in steady state because the dynamic rise erases it after one free.
Per-decade means (mean of per-size medians, endpoint touch, ns/op; full per-size table in the summariser output):
  decade        mimir     glibc    glmap   glarena   gltrim
  1-8 KiB      1538.40      7.00  1711.11      6.25      6.95   (the mimir mean includes the 1024 span row at 23.98; mapping rows alone are 1911-2282)
  16-64 KiB    2509.58      8.71  2154.01      7.76      8.82
  128-512 KiB  2832.96      8.13  2422.78      8.06   1403.15   (gltrim mean mixes its sub-threshold rows with its trim-cliff rows - see below)
  1-4 MiB      3695.28      8.26  3011.47      8.18   3011.19
  8-32 MiB     3981.47   1100.26  3142.09      9.33   3344.76   (the glibc mean is its 32 MiB cap row, 3282.89, averaged with three ~9 ns rows)
The mapping price is nearly size-flat: 1911 ns at 2048 rising only to 4011 ns at 33554432 (2.1x over a 16384x size range). Its split is stable in shape (Z-023 decomposition, this run): the bare pair is ~980-1080 ns at every size; the fault+populated-teardown term grows from ~503 ns at 2048 to ~2362 ns at 33554432 and passes the pair from 16 KiB upward; the bookkeeping residual is 401-840 ns at every size. MimirAlloc also pays MORE than glibc's own mapping path at every size (glmap is 1508-3220 across the ladder, always below mimir): the premium is the reserve-page fault (mimir faults 3.000-3.025 times per op - reserve page + endpoint pages - against glmap's ~1.0 at small sizes, where glibc's header shares the first user page) plus the global lock, descriptor publication and the free-side registry walk (Z-024: the walk is the free path). MimirAlloc's per-op waste bytes (mapping_length - request: reserve page + page round-up) fall from 6144 B (300%) at 2048 to 4096 B (0.01%) at 33554432 - waste is a small-size scandal and a large-size rounding error.
WHERE THE MAPPING PATH STARTS PAYING: on bytes-served terms the fixed ~1.4 us of pair+bookkeeping is amortised as size grows - cost per requested byte falls from 0.93 ns/B at 2048 to 0.00012 ns/B at 33554432 - and the mapping buys exact-size memory, immediate return to the OS and zero retention at every size. It never wins on time against a retaining allocator at any measured size; its value case is memory honesty (Z-006's retention leg is where that case is graded), not speed.
WHERE IT STARTS BLEEDING - three named places:
  (1) The decade just above the cutoff (1025..~8192): the full mapping price for blocks a span nearly serves, at 300-50% waste - the worst value zone in the study, and the zone a raised cutoff or a small-mapping cache reaches first.
  (2) The middle decades against steady glibc (128 KiB..16 MiB): ratios 315-486x, because glibc's dynamic rise shelters exactly this range while every MimirAlloc op re-pays pair+faults. This is KL-C's home ground, widened from Z-023's batch finding (4.16x in the 32-live batch shape) to the single-block shape measured here.
  (3) Dense touch at 1 MiB and 8 MiB (opfull): mimir re-faults every user page on every allocation - 106329.56 ns/op at 1048576 (257.0 faults/op, 38.07x vs glibc-steady) and 854712.75 at 8388608 (2049.1 faults/op, 34.52x). The bleed scales with pages touched, without bound in this range. At 33554432 the dense ratio COLLAPSES to 1.01x (4022825.00 vs 3992785.50, both ~8193 faults/op): above glibc's cap cliff both allocators map-and-fault every time, and MimirAlloc is at parity - the only place in the study where it is.
THE TRIM LESSON (a finding about reuse itself, priced for Z-047): reuse only pays if freed memory is RETAINED. gltrim - arena-forced but with glibc's default 128 KiB trim threshold - costs ~8.8 ns/op up to 131072 and then jumps to 2061-3621 ns/op at 262144 and above (indicative leg, see load note): every large free shrinks the arena top back to the OS, and the next allocation re-pays the kernel. Raising the trim threshold alongside (glarena) restores ~8-10 ns/op at every size. glibc's steady leg escapes the cliff because the dynamic rise moves its trim threshold with its mmap threshold. For the mapping-cache design this is the controlling fact: the pool's retention bound (bytes and count, Z-047) IS the price - a pool that returns mappings too eagerly is the mapping path with extra bookkeeping.

Model: a request at or below the candidate cutoff is priced at the glarena median (retention-reuse proxy, ~8 ns/op); above it, at the measured op/mimir median. Two mixes: uniform over the 16-size ladder, and the K-002 large_batch mix (2048, 8192, 16384, 65536, 262144, 1048576, equal weight). Sensitivity: the proxy is glibc's arena, not a built MimirAlloc reuse path. Charging a hypothetical served size the measured bookkeeping residual on top (+500 ns/op - deliberately above the 401-440 ns residual at most sizes) still leaves: cutoff 131072 uniform 2023.34 (-29.1%), large_batch 1303.06 (-48.0%); cutoff 1048576 uniform 1588.37 (-44.4%), large_batch 507.83 (-79.7%); cutoff 33554432 uniform 507.81 (-82.2%). The direction and rough scale of every row survive the proxy's correction; the last digit does not, and is not claimed.
What a raised cutoff would actually require (priced options are options, not implementations): the span machinery serves one page per span, so no constant change reaches past 4096 - sizes above a page need either multi-page spans (new geometry, new fragmentation questions for Z-055's line) or the Z-047/Z-048 mapping cache, which keeps the 1024 cutoff and instead attacks the pair term (~1.0 us of the ~1.9-4.0 us) plus the reserve-page fault on cache hits, while leaving first-touch user faults where Z-014 put them. The two roads are priced against each other by this table: a perfect mapping cache caps its saving near the pair+reserve share (about 55-75% of the single-block price at small sizes, less at large), while a served-path cutoff at 131072 is worth 37.2% uniform / 61.3% large_batch in the ideal-proxy model and ~29% / ~48% with bookkeeping charged. Z-047's design should be written against both ceilings.
