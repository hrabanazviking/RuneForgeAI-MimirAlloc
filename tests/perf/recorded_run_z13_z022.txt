=== VERDICT - WHERE THE SMALL-PATH CYCLES GO ===
One function owns nearly half the small path. find_span_live_locked (the free path's span search + validation, src/core/mimir_core.c:1800-1916) takes a 43.14% median share of ALL cycles on small_churn, and the validation helper it inlines, span_header_is_live (:1279-1310), adds a further 4.27% in its non-inlined calls: the free-path validation complex is ~47% of the path. The reason is structural, and it is the headline Wave 4 must burn down: a small free searches for its span TWICE - mimir_core_free calls find_span_live_locked, and small_free_locked then calls it again on the same pointer (mimir_core.c:3117 and :2014) - and on every walk, every span visited is fully revalidated (the six-field header check plus the J-006 relation set, including a 64-bit DIVISION in span_capacity at :1250, 3.29% of all cycles by itself) before the free-list double-free walk even starts. The lock complex is second: pthread_mutex_lock + unlock + the L6 wrapper pair + PLT stubs sum to ~19.2% of cycles, for a lock Z-019 measured held only 60-86 cycles uncontended on this path - the per-op lock tax is paid in call overhead and atomic traffic, not in waiting. The alloc side is comparatively lean (small_alloc_locked 10.75%: the class_index_for linear scan over up to 20 classes inlined at its head, the active-span hit, the free-list pop / carve) and small_free_locked's own body is 9.46%, inside which the retention walk empty_spans_of_class_locked (:1507-1528) fires on EVERY small_churn free - single-block churn takes its span to live_count 0 every time, so the walk over all spans of the class runs per free (srcline :1512/:1513/:1523 sum to ~6.1%). The mapping-registry walk that mimir_core_free performs first (find_candidate_locked) does not appear above the 0.3% limit on this workload: with no large blocks live its list is empty and the walk is one NULL check - it is NOT a small-path cost, whatever it costs the large path (Z-023's question).

=== WHAT WAVE 4 OWNS FROM THIS TABLE ===
- Z-028/Z-029 (TLS cache): a cache hit removes rows 1, 3, 6, 8 AND the lock rows 4, 5, 9, 10 from the per-op path at once - ~76% of the measured cycles are lock-or-validation work a TLS hit never performs. This table is the before-picture the TLS verdict (Z-031) is judged against.
- The double span search (verdict, finding 1) is a plain defect of economy inside the current core, independent of the TLS redesign: any core-level free-path cleanup must keep the two walks' combined share as its regression measure.
- span_capacity's division (:1250) and the full J-006 revalidation per visited span are correctness armour priced per call; a redesign that validates once per span-state change, or caches capacity, retires 3.29% + most of the 23.68% line-region validation share - at a proof burden Z-026 must price honestly (the armour exists because J-006 corruption cases are real).
