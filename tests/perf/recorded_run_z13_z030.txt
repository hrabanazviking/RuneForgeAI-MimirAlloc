THE QUEUE (tests/concurrency/mimir_remote_queue_z030.h, header-only, test-only like every internal surface in this tree):
- Bounded MPSC ring, capacity 256 pointers per owner. Ground: a Z-028 bin holds 16 blocks per class and the banked mixed_batch shape cycles 256 live blocks; 256 holds one full workload burst per owner and keeps the suite's exact-fill test cheap and deterministic. A bound for testability and burst coverage, not an optimality claim.
- Push (any thread): claim the head slot by CAS only after observing its sequence stamp free for this cycle; publish by release-storing stamp = index+1 after the pointer store. FULL is a returned, counted outcome - the caller keeps its block and frees it itself (the bypass road); a queue that silently dropped a pointer would leak the block. CLOSED likewise (after mrq_close).
- Drain (owner only): FIFO from the tail, at most MRQ_DRAIN_BATCH = 8 pointers per call - the Z-028 batch constant, so a future Z-029 drain loop inherits the contract's batch discipline. Acquire stamp load before the pointer read; slot re-stamped free for the next cycle (stamp = index + capacity). Sequence stamps are monotone cycle counters: slot-reuse ABA cannot occur.
- Close/exit protocol: mrq_close stops new pushes (counted closed_rejects); items queued before close remain drainable; the queue retires at pending 0. There is deliberately NO wakeup channel: draining is explicit/polling, so no lost-wakeup race exists to test or to have.
- False sharing (Z-016, contract S5): head + producer counters on their own 64-byte line, tail + owner counters on another; the suite's Q0 phase asserts the offsets. Per-slot stamps are the unavoidable one-word-per-block hand-off, which is payload, not metadata contention.
- Accounting at quiescence: pushed == drained + pending, and overflow/closed_rejects count every refused push. The suite asserts these identities in every geometry.

VERDICTS (roadmap Law 2, honest grading; all comparisons are same-run, same-shape):
  Freer-side cost: WIN for the queue. Enqueueing a remote free costs the freer 8-11% less than freeing directly in the pair geometries (408.2 vs 444.6 same-CCD; 444.4 vs 481.9 cross-CCD) and ~20% less under swap contention (1022.5 vs 1280.4; 1285.4 vs 1606.3). The freer never takes the central lock and never walks a registry.
  Total system cost, pair geometries: LOSS for the queue, +35.9% same-CCD (604.1 vs 444.6) and +60.9% cross-CCD (775.4 vs 481.9). The queue does not remove the dominant cost - the owner still pays the same registry walks under the same central lock when it drains (Z-024 anatomy point 3 predicted exactly this: "a remote-free queue routed to the owner thread does NOT, by itself, buy back the dominant cost"). It adds a hand-off on top. On the CURRENT core (no TLS cache, no per-block owner record, lookup-by-walk) a remote-free queue is a freer-side latency device, not a throughput device.
  Total system cost, swap x4: TIE. 1274.4 vs 1280.4 same-CCD (-0.5%) and 1636.2 vs 1606.3 cross-CCD (+1.9%) - inside the runs' own drift. Under 4-way concurrent remote frees the queue's hand-off cost and the direct path's lock convoy cost price out the same.
  What this means for Wave 4, stated plainly: the queue earns its place ONLY as part of the Z-029 design, where the owner's drain feeds its own TLS bins (a drained block becomes a cache hit, not a central-core free) and where Z-031's verdict run can price the assembled path. Priced against today's core, as this gate required, it is a proven freer-side WIN, a pair-geometry total LOSS, and a swap TIE - and the loss is attributed, not hidden: the registry walk is the remaining wound, and killing it (lookup-by-arithmetic) is a contract trade for a later slice to record, exactly as Z-024 left it.
