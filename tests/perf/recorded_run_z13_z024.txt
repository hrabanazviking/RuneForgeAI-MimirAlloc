method (per docs/Z13_MEASUREMENT_PROTOCOL.md, Z-004): each phase is run as separate PROCESSES, 3 warm-up processes discarded + 7 measured per phase per invocation, 25 phases x 7 = 175 RESULT lines per invocation. Headline = MEDIAN across processes of per-process ns/op (summarize_z024.py); per-phase split-half drift within invocation 1 is <= 6.81% for every group, most <= 2%. Inside a process, ONE batch is timed with CLOCK_MONOTONIC around ONLY the free loop; allocation, pattern-touch and checksum verification all happen outside the timed region. The allocator writes a per-block pattern (first and last byte); the FREEING side verifies the batch checksum before the timer starts (K-002 discipline); exactly one checksum per (leg, phase, size) group in both invocations (verified by the summariser, exit 0). Threads pin themselves with pthread_setaffinity_np (CCD0 = CPUs 0-7, CCD1 = CPUs 8-15); local phases also run under taskset -c 2. Remote handoff is a preallocated pointer array plus padded atomic sequence words: no allocation, no mutex, no condvar in the handoff, and the consumer's timer starts only after it observes the publish flag, so handoff cost is excluded from the free cost. Swap phases use a two-stage release so victim-checksum verification also completes before the timed wall window opens.

geometries and shapes:
  local          allocating thread == freeing thread (CPU 2).
  remote         producer allocates on one CPU, a single consumer frees on another: same-CCD pair 2 -> 3, cross-CCD pair 2 -> 10, and the reversed cross-CCD pair 10 -> 2.
  swap           4 threads each allocate a batch, then each frees the NEXT thread's batch concurrently (steady half); headline is wall time over all frees (throughput cost per free under contention). Pairs: CPUs 2,3,4,5 (same-CCD) and 2,10,3,11 (every free crosses a CCD).
  steady         free every SECOND block of the batch: every span keeps live blocks, no span is released; the timed cost is lock + lookup + free-list threading only.
  emptying       free ALL blocks: small spans empty out and all but the retention bound (2 per class) are released whole inside the timed region; mapping frees always munmap.
  local_rev      emptying but frees run NEWEST-first: the newest blocks sit in the head span / head mapping descriptor, so registry walks are shortest. Paired with oldest-first local_emptying it isolates the walk-depth share.
  Batch shapes: small 8,192 blocks (64-byte class: 62 objects/span -> 133 spans live; 512-byte class: 7 objects/span -> 1,171 spans live); mapping 2,048 blocks at 8,192 bytes (2,048 live mappings) and 512 blocks at 262,144 bytes (512 live mappings). Forward (allocation-order) frees free the OLDEST blocks first - the deepest registry positions. Blocks are pattern-touched at first and last byte only; for mapping blocks that faults exactly 2 pages per mapping, so the munmap page-table share in the mapping numbers is a FLOOR (a fully-touched large block costs more to unmap; Z-014/Z-015 bound that share).

ANATOMY - where the free time goes (source: src/core/mimir_core.c on this tree):
1. THE REGISTRY WALK IS THE FREE PATH. mimir_core_free first walks the mapping registry (find_candidate_locked, linear in live mappings), then walks the span registry (find_span_live_locked, linear in live spans) - and small_free_locked then calls find_span_live_locked a SECOND time, repeating the registry walk and the found span's free-list scan. A small free therefore pays two linear walks whose length is set by how many spans/mappings are live and how deep the block's owner sits in the list (registry inserts at the head; allocation order frees the oldest = deepest first). The reverse-order pairs price the walk directly:
     64-byte emptying:   oldest-first 497.5 vs newest-first 75.1 ns  (walk-depth share ~85%)
     512-byte emptying:  oldest-first 6,333.5 vs newest-first 523.2 ns (walk-depth share ~92%)
     8192 mapping:       oldest-first 6,390.9 vs newest-first 1,176.0 ns (walk-depth share ~82%)
   The 512-byte class is the extreme case by arithmetic, not by class policy: 7 objects per span means the 8,192-block batch keeps 1,171 spans live (vs 133 at 64 bytes), so the average walk is ~9x longer and the free costs ~12.8x the 64-byte steady free (5,668.0 vs 444.1). Free cost on this core is a function of LIVE SET SIZE and FREE ORDER, not of the block's size class per se. Mixed-order real churn lands between the oldest-first and newest-first regimes; both bounds are now of record.
2. THE LOCK ITSELF IS NOT THE STORY (single-threaded). Z-019 measured the free lock hold at 86 cycles (~30 ns at this host's TSC rate) for the small path - an order below the 444 ns steady free above. The gap between the Z-019 hold and this slice's per-free cost is the walk work done INSIDE that hold at these live-set depths (Z-019's workloads keep far fewer spans live). Any Wave 4/5 redesign that only cheapens locking leaves the dominant term standing.
3. REMOTE FREE IS CHEAP - UNTIL IT IS CONCURRENT. A single remote consumer freeing a quiescent batch pays only +2.7% (same CCD) to +8.9% (cross CCD) over local free on the small path: the walk and the lock dwarf the cache-transfer cost of the freed block's first word and the span header, and the reversed cross-CCD pair (+3.6%) shows no CCD-direction asymmetry. On the mapping path the remote premium is real money (+15.2% same-CCD, +31.6% cross-CCD at 8,192; +77.2% cross-CCD at 262,144 - the freeing thread unmaps in its own context and the metadata/page-table lines come from the producer's CCD; mechanism attribution is interpretation, the premium itself is measured). Under CONCURRENT remote frees (swap x4) the one global lock serialises everything: wall cost per free rises to 2.54x (same-CCD) / 3.06x (cross-CCD) the local steady figure at only 4 threads.
   Consequence for Z-026/Z-030, stated plainly: a remote-free queue routed to the owner thread does NOT, by itself, buy back the dominant cost - the owner would still pay the same registry walks under the same lock. The evidence argues the redesign must first kill the walk (the span of a small block is mechanically derivable - spans are exactly one page with the header at the page base - but the registry walk is also the J-006 integrity posture, so replacing lookup-by-walk with lookup-by-arithmetic is a contract trade Z-026 must record, not a free optimisation). Queueing helps the swap-geometry contention term, not the walk term.
4. SPAN RELEASE (emptying minus steady, local): +53.3 ns/free at 64 bytes (+12.0%) and +665.5 ns/free at 512 bytes (+11.7%). The share is roughly constant in proportion because release count scales with span count (130 of 133 spans released at 64 bytes; 1,169 of 1,171 at 512) - the per-release munmap is amortised over the objects the span held (62 vs 7), which is exactly why the absolute delta is 12x larger for the coarse class.
5. MAPPING FREE AT SHALLOW DEPTH (newest-first, 1,176.0 ns at 8,192) is the mapping path's floor at this batch shape: short walk + descriptor validation + munmap of a 3-page mapping with 2 faulted pages, all under the lock. Forward-order mapping frees at depth are walk-dominated (point 1), and the 262,144 local figure (2,616.5) sitting BELOW the 8,192 figure (6,390.9) is the same effect - 512 live mappings walk shorter than 2,048 - not a size effect; with fully-touched blocks the munmap share grows (see the shape caveat above) and Z-023's large-path split owns that term.
6. TELEMETRY GAP CARRIED, NOT NEW: cross_thread_frees (J-002) counts only mapping-path remote frees (INC-J002-01, PARTIAL by design - no per-object owner record exists for small blocks). This slice's small remote frees are therefore measurable only by timing, never by the stats surface; Z-026 should treat small-path remote-free accounting as unbuilt, not as zero.

  Small-path free vs glibc: LOSS, large. Local steady 444.1 vs 4.6 ns (~96x); remote cross-CCD steady 470.0 vs 13.4 ns (~35x). glibc's remote premium is proportionally bigger (+~190%) but absolutely tiny (+8.8 ns); mimir's is proportionally small (+5.8%) because the walk already dwarfs transfer costs.
  Mapping-path free vs glibc at 8,192: NOT LIKE-FOR-LIKE (glibc heap vs mimir munmap, see comparator table) - no verdict claimed from that row. The mapping path's own anatomy (walk ~82%, floor 1,176.0 ns shallow) is the recorded result.
  Remote-free premium on the banked core: SMALL for a single consumer (<= +8.9% small path), REAL on the mapping path (up to +77.2% cross-CCD at 262,144), and SEVERE under concurrency (2.54x..3.06x wall at 4 threads, lock serialisation).
