=== METHOD ===
The harness has three parts. probe_z056.c is the soak probe: one source, two legs (mimir = this tree's banked allocator with the Z-048 mapping cache and Z-049 THP posture live; glibc = host malloc/free/calloc/realloc). run_z056.sh is the runner with the detached-run discipline the roadmap demands - start builds both legs and launches them detached (setsid + nohup), poll reports liveness and sample counts without blocking, collect refuses while a leg is still running and otherwise verifies both exit codes and runs the summariser. summarize_z056.py checks sample completeness (the gate quantity) and prints the drift series.
The soak workload is a mixed lifecycle over a 320-slot registry. Sizes come from one fixed-seed xorshift PRNG: 55% small (16..1024 B), 25% medium (4 KiB..64 KiB), 20% large (128 KiB..2 MiB). Every block is dense-touched (full memset; the calloc fills - 1 in 8 small fills - are verified zero before stamping) so RSS is real resident memory. Each slot carries a TTL of 100..20000 operations; the free-running loop replaces expired or empty slots, and additionally applies random realloc operations (15% of visits - contents-preservation up to the smaller size is verified on every realloc, then the block is re-stamped) and random free/refill operations (15%).
Sampling, every interval_s of wall clock plus t=0 and the deadline: verify the first, middle and last stored byte of every live block (any mismatch aborts the process - corruption evidence, not a statistic); read /proc/self/smaps_rollup (Rss + Pss, the Z-006 method) and /proc/self/maps (VMA count, allocation-VMA window holes, the Z-053 definition); run a timed latency batch (256 x 64 B alloc/free + 32 x 64 KiB alloc/free, CLOCK_MONOTONIC) reported as ns/op. After the deadline the probe frees everything and emits DRAINED (post-drain Rss/Pss/VMA) and DONE (total ops, sample count, peak Rss).
Time-boxed, not op-boxed (stated plainly): the two legs run the same duration and the same workload DISTRIBUTION, not the identical operation sequence, so per-sample checksums are per-process integrity evidence only and are never compared across legs. The legs ran CONCURRENTLY, pinned to separate cores (mimir taskset -c 2, glibc taskset -c 3), because RSS/PSS are per-process reads; the concurrency caveat falls on the latency batches, which are shared-machine samples (see F5).
Pilot parameters (the roadmap gate run): 600 seconds, 10-second interval, 61 scheduled samples per leg (t = 0, 10, ..., 600). A 20-second smoke run (3 samples per leg, COMPLETE, collect exit 0) preceded the pilot and proved start/poll/collect end to end. The full one-hour run is Z-057's job and was NOT run here; the harness default is 3600 s / 60 s for it.

Gate: "harness proven on a 10-minute pilot with complete samples" - MET. F1 - Throughput under the identical distribution differs 6.3x. In 600 s the glibc leg executed 271,437,568 registry operations against the mimir leg's 43,181,056 (6.29x). The soak therefore stresses glibc with far more lifecycle events per unit time; per-leg drift curves are comparable as resident-shape series (live_bytes medians are close: mimir 74,237,019 B, glibc 76,236,239 B) but NOT as equal-work measurements. Any Z-057/Z-061 soak comparison inherits this caveat.
F2 - Resident shape over the pilot: mimir holds LESS resident memory per live byte than glibc in this mixed lifecycle. net_factor ((Rss - Rss_t0) / live_bytes) median 2.016 for mimir vs 2.522 for glibc; worst sample 2.638 vs 3.275. Peak Rss: mimir 174,744 KiB vs glibc 198,468 KiB, on live peaks of 99,199,308 B vs 93,316,545 B. This does not contradict Z-053's holed-phase LOSS: Z-053 measured a single adversarial free pattern at a frozen phase; the soak's continuous mixed lifecycle (TTL expiry, realloc, refill across all classes) is a different workload, and on it the banked allocator's resident factor is the lower one across all 61 samples' medians. Grade in roadmap words for the soak workload: WIN on resident shape (this workload, this machine, this window).
F3 - Drain retention is the mirror image of Z-053's checker/uniform rows and is allocator-policy, not leakage: after freeing all 320 blocks in-process, mimir retains 36,100 KiB net Rss across +83 VMAs - at the Z-048 mapping-cache design bounds (64 mappings / 32 MiB, plus small-path spans) - while glibc retains 194,444 KiB net (its DRAINED Rss 196,528 KiB equals its final live sample: in-process, freed large blocks sit in arena/top retention under the dynamic mmap threshold and are not returned until exit or trim). Both figures are in-process readings taken the instant after the drain; process exit returns everything for both legs. Z-057's full-hour report should keep the DRAINED line for exactly this comparison.
F4 - Fragmentation window: mimir's allocation-VMA window carries a median of 109 holes (median 53,063,680 hole bytes, worst largest hole 20,877,312 B) at every live sample - the interleaved size mix keeps its address window permanently holed, consistent with Z-053 F2 (MimirAlloc trades address holes for pooled retention). glibc's window shows 0 holes at every sample: its live blocks sit in merged/contiguous VMAs, and its fragmentation cost appears instead as the higher net_factor of F2. Address holes and resident holes remain different quantities; the harness now records both, per sample, for the full hour.
F5 - Latency samples (shared-machine, NOT baselines): small-batch (64 B) median 238 ns/op mimir vs 44 glibc (LOSS ~5.4x); large-batch (64 KiB) median 2,290 ns/op mimir vs 135 glibc (LOSS ~17x). The t=260 sample spikes in both legs simultaneously (mimir 564 / 4,670; glibc 84 / 347) - a co-tenant event crossing both pinned cores, kept in the series as the honest shared-machine fingerprint; medians are insensitive to it. These batch figures are soak-health samples (is the allocator degrading over time?) - and on that question the series is flat for both legs from t=10 to t=600, no drift trend. Z-057 inherits the same reading discipline.
F6 - Integrity: neither leg aborted. Every sample of both legs verified the stored bytes of all 320 live blocks (first/middle/last), every calloc fill verified zeroed, every realloc verified contents-preserved - 61 samples x 2 legs with zero mismatches, and both probes exited 0. No allocator bug was discovered by the pilot.
