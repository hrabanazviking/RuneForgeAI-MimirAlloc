=== VERDICT - THE ANATOMY ===

CALLOC, small path (16..1024 B, recycled): MimirAlloc
is FLAT 57-125 ns/op and glibc is 2.6-14.0 ns/op:
LOSS at every class, 9x-23x. But the anatomy acquits
zeroing: MimirAlloc's zeroing premium (CALLOC minus
MALLOC, same leg) is only 0.6-5.1 ns across the small
classes, and its calloc matches its own malloc+memset
within ~5 ns - the explicit usable-extent memset is
nearly free at these sizes. The entire small-calloc
loss is the allocator path itself (the one global
lock + span machinery; compare Z-019: calloc lock
hold median 65 cycles ~ 22 ns of this ~59 ns total).
glibc's small-calloc premium is 0-11 ns (tcache).

CLIFF 1 - THE 1024 CUTOFF (named size: 1025 B).
MimirAlloc CALLOC steps from 124.75 ns at 1024 B to
2,078.56 ns at 1025 B (16.7x; run 1: 134.11 ->
4,754.19, 35x). The MALLOC leg steps identically
(120.60 -> 1,901.50), proving the cliff is the
mapping-per-allocation path itself (mmap + fault +
zero + munmap per operation), NOT zeroing: the
calloc-over-malloc premium at 1025 B is only ~177 ns
of the 2,079. glibc shows NO step at 1025 (14.02 ->
13.68 ns; its arena absorbs the size). BULK repeats
the cliff (1,049.45 ns at 1024 -> 2,273.03 at 1025).
Every MimirAlloc size above the cutoff sits on the
mapping plateau: calloc 2,018 ns at 4096, 8,937 at
65536, 16,711 at 131072, 119,775 at 1048576 - LOSS
at every size (37x-152x vs glibc).

CLIFF 2 - LARGE-CALLOC ZEROING (named sizes: 65536 B
and 1048576 B). Above the cutoff the zeroing premium
explodes: calloc minus malloc for MimirAlloc is
~177 ns at 1025 B, ~6,426 ns at 65536 B and
~116,566 ns at 1048576 B. Cause, from the source
(src/core/mimir_core.c, B-008 S3): calloc zeroes the
whole usable extent EXPLICITLY, so on the mapping
path it memsets a fresh kernel-zero mapping and pays
first-touch faults on every page (~468 ns/page at
1 MiB, consistent with the Z-015 fault curve) for
zeroing the kernel already did. glibc's large calloc
rides the kernel zero pages instead: 6,372 ns at
1 MiB (an 18.8x WIN for glibc - the single largest
calloc gap in the table). A Wave-4 calloc that skips
the memset for fresh mappings closes most of this.

REALLOC LADDER - copy vs extend, by the moved
fraction (load-independent, exact): MimirAlloc STAYS
(moved 0.00) iff the new size fits the block's span
class - within-class grows (17->32, 100->112,
1008->1024), same-size, and ALL shrinks stay, at
26-222 ns. EVERY grow beyond the class MOVES
(moved 1.00): alloc + copy of the old class extent +
free under the one lock, 121-753 ns inside the small
classes. Crossing the cutoff moving (1023->1025,
1024->1025) costs 1,324-1,351 ns. On the mapping
path every grow moves and copies: 2,208 ns
(2048->4096) rising to 35,044 ns (262144->1048576,
DRIFT-REJECT flagged, run 1 read 164,407 ns under
heavier load - direction and order of magnitude
agree; the value is indicative).
glibc EXTENDS in place for most grows (moved 0.00:
48->112, 112->256, 512->896, all cutoff crossings,
and every large grow - mremap: 262144->1048576 at
60.73 ns vs MimirAlloc's 35,044: 577x, the largest
realloc gap in the table). glibc moves only where
its bins force it (16->48, 256->512, 2048->4096,
4096->16384, and 1008->1024 - the ladder's single
WIN row: MimirAlloc stays in class at 26.44 ns,
glibc moves at 32.80 ns).
Net realloc grade: LOSS on 22 of 23 pairs, TIE on
none, WIN on 1 (1008->1024). Stay/shrink rows lose
narrowly (26-43 ns vs 19-33 ns - the lock/path tax);
grow rows lose by the copy, and large grows by the
copy plus faults plus munmap, against an opponent
that mostly does not copy at all.

Correctness held throughout: 924 zero-verification
