=== VERDICT - THE RECYCLED-VS-FRESH RATIO ===
Headline ratio (median across all sizes, run 1): recyc_map/fresh 0.999, recyc_keep/fresh 0.979. Large sizes >=16 MiB: recyc_map 0.999, recyc_keep 0.988 (run 1); run 2 confirms 0.999 / 0.997 at large sizes. Per the protocol section 7 noise bound (5%), a median difference inside 5% is WITHIN NOISE: at the sizes the large path actually faults (16 MiB..1 GiB), RECYCLING DOES NOT MAKE FAULTS CHEAPER ON THIS MACHINE. Replacement pages cost the same ~460-505 ns/page to fault in whether they come from a brand-new mapping, a re-mmap of just-freed space, or a retained pooled mapping re-faulted after MADV_DONTNEED.
Proof the recycled legs really re-fault: median minor faults per page = 1.000 in EVERY leg at EVERY size - recycled mappings hold no hidden resident pages; every page is paid for again, in full.
The one real discount is mid-size: at 262144 bytes the recycled legs run 0.91x fresh in run 1 and 0.89x in run 2 (both modes, reproducible, outside noise), and at 1 MiB ~0.96x in both runs - recently freed pages at cache-resident scales are slightly cheaper. By 16 MiB the discount is gone (0.97-0.99x, inside noise) and by 64 MiB it is exactly 1.00x. Fault cost per page rises with size in all legs: ~345-375 ns/page at 256 KiB..4 MiB, 379 at 16 MiB, 466 at 64 MiB, 485 at 256 MiB, 505 at 1 GiB - at large sizes the cost is dominated by page zeroing + page-table work at memory bandwidth, which no recycling scheme avoids.
What recycling DOES save (separate columns, run 1 medians): mmap itself is ~0.5-6.5 us at all sizes (noise), but munmap of a faulted mapping costs ~104 ns/page at 1 GiB (27.3 ms total) and ~78 ns/page at 256 MiB - a retained pooled mapping (recyc_keep) avoids repeated mmap/munmap/VMA churn, and the pooled legs own discard step is not free either: MADV_DONTNEED costs 69 ns/page at 64 MiB, 74 ns/page at 256 MiB and 103 ns/page at 1 GiB (run 1 medians, aux column in the raw data) - the same order as munmap, since both zap page tables. So the KL-C large path lives or dies by this: pooling mappings is worth it for syscall/VMA economics (Z-015 territory), but NO fault-cost saving may be booked for it - a design that assumes recycled mappings fault cheaper at large sizes is designing on a number this machine does not have.
Small-size honesty: at 4-64 KiB the total touch times are 0.4-24 us, spreads reach 130% and drift 29% on individual cells; the ratios there (0.87-1.13 across runs) are timer noise and no claim is made from them. Every size >=256 KiB cell in both runs has spread <=25% and, except run 1 recyc_map at 64 KiB (a sub-6 us cell), drift <=7%.
