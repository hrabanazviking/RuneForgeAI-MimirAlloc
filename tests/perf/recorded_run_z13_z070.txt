S2. Mechanism (src/core/mimir_core.c/.h)
MEMBER: a second free of a small block is refused.
  - TLS course (the gap this slice closes): tls_free_local
    scans the calling thread's own per-class bin (at most
    MIMIR_TLS_BIN_CAPACITY pointer compares against a
    thread-local array, no lock) before binning; a hit
    tallies and reports, and the free returns before any
    accounting moves.
  - Central courses: the J-006 free-list walk inside
    find_span_mode_locked already met the pointer on its own
    span's free list (that cost predates this slice); the
    walk now labels the meeting (thread-local flag) and
    mimir_core_free consumes the label after unlocking:
    z070_df_report tallies once per refused central free.
  A block drained from a bin to the central list loses its
  owner record with the drain, so a later second free routes
  central and is refused there -- every residence is covered
  exactly once.
POISON: at every small free course, bytes [8, class_size)
  of the freed block are filled with 0xDF (the first 8 bytes
  are the free-list link's home, never poisoned or
  verified); at every reuse handout (central free-list pop,
  TLS bin pop, TLS refill take) the range is verified
  word-wise. A mismatch tallies and reports; the handout
  PROCEEDS (telemetry posture: the signal is about caller
  data, not allocator metadata). Fresh carves are poisoned
  as they enter a bin (tls_take_one_locked), so a
  never-freed block cannot fail its first verify.
Tallies + wiring: struct mimir_stats gains
  double_free_detections and poison_detections APPENDED at
  the end (Z-064 append discipline), copied by the snapshot
  and readable lock-free via
  mimir_core_double_free_detections() /
  mimir_core_double_free_poison_detections() (shape (b),
  bound local; nm -D on libmimiralloc.so stays exactly 4,
  verified). Diag causes appended: DOUBLE_FREE (7),
  POISON (8); no existing enumerator, name, or offset moves.
Scope exclusions, by design: the guard is off under
  MIMIR_DEBUG (the J-005 poison/quarantine owns detection)
  and under MIMIR_OOL_META (the Z-034 no-block-byte
  invariant owns its spans); large mapping-path blocks are
  out of scope (S6 leg 4).

S3. The economics ruling (why poison is capped)
Table A (S4) costed FULL-EXTENT poison. Membership is cheap
everywhere (+0.25..+2.27 ns/pair in both tables). Poison is
not: its cost grows linearly with class size -- +8.0 ns/pair
at class 64, +67.6 at 256, +222.0 at 1024 (8.8x the whole
pair) -- while its detection value does not grow:
use-after-free writes land at the start of the block in the
common case. Full-extent poison therefore FAILED its
pre-registered adoption bars at classes 64/256/1024 and at
churnN/churnF class 64 (S4) and was REJECTED in that form.
Adopted form: the poisoned range is capped at
MIMIR_DFREE_POISON_SPAN = 32 bytes past the link word --
bytes [8, 40) of every class, the first 40 bytes of the
block. Table B (S5) re-measured the as-shipped capped build
against the same pre-registered bars: ALL NINE BARS PASS,
full cost flat at +0.9..+4.6 ns/pair from class 16 through
class 1024. The cap boundary is a tested scope limit, not a
silent one (S6, poison leg boundary portion: a write at
offset 40 is NOT detected, by design, and the test pins it).
