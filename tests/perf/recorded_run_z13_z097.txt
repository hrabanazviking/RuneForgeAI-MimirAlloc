=== VERDICT ===
Per-process tax vs glibc: LOSS on every shape (headline = invocation 1 medians). A process that never allocates pays +25,154 ns (+16.98%, 1.17x) just for the interposition .so load. One allocation raises the tax to +41,474 ns (+26.72%, 1.27x): the first-allocation increment over the no-alloc leg is 23,354 ns under MimirAlloc vs 7,034 ns under glibc, a first-touch tax of +16,320 ns beyond the load tax. Eight allocations across the class ladder (16..262144, the top rung on the mapping path) pay +112,804 ns (+71.08%, 1.71x). A working short-lived process (1000 small alloc/touch/free cycles) pays +202,966 ns (+130.25%, 2.30x). Storm projection: at churn shape the tax is +203 ms per 1,000 processes and +2.03 s per 10,000 - the concrete price on shell scripts and build farms that fork+exec thousands of short-lived children. REASON NAMED (Law 2): two stacked terms - (1) a fixed per-process load/init term (+25.2 us on the none leg, with preload minor faults 84.4 vs glibc 73.0 per process: the extra .so mapping, relocation and allocator initialisation every exec pays whether or not it allocates), and (2) the per-allocation small-path cost documented since Z-003/Z-024 (registry/span walks), which the increments isolate: +16,320 ns for the first touch, +87,650 ns for the eight ladder allocations, +177,812 ns for the 1000 churn pairs, with preload minor faults per process rising 84 (none) to 234 (churn) vs a flat 73-79 under glibc. The tax is therefore not a load-time-only curiosity: it grows with exactly the work a short-lived process does.
