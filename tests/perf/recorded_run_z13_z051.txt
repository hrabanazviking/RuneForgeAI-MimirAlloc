VERDICT - Z-051
Success cost (table above; medians of 5 processes):
- Fully touched (the honest huge-allocation price: alloc
  + first-touch of every page + free): TIE at every size,
  mimir/glibc total ratios 0.995..1.018. The price is the
  kernel's, not the allocator's: first touch runs
  ~486-496 ns/page at both ends of the ladder, minor
  faults equal pages+2 in both legs, RSS after touch is
  size + ~2.3 MB baseline in both legs, and free of a
  touched block costs ~70 ns/page (256 MiB) rising to
  ~89 ns/page (8 GiB) in both legs - page-table teardown
  of a fresh mapping, identical because both allocators
  hand the kernel the same single fresh mapping.
- Untouched (alloc + free only): LOSS at every size,
  ratios 1.10x-1.22x, on totals of only 5-10 microseconds
  (alloc ~3 us, free ~3-4 us per leg). MimirAlloc pays
  roughly 0.5-1.5 us more per alloc/free pair than glibc:
  the global lock, descriptor write, registry insert and
  checked counter updates, against glibc's mmap-chunk
  path. Several untouched legs carry drift above the 8%
  bound (microsecond totals on a shared host), so the
  magnitudes are indicative; the direction was identical
  at all six sizes in two independent runs.
- UMA behaviour: one NUMA node, no locality effects to
  pay. An untouched huge allocation is pure virtual
  space in both legs: RSS stays at the ~2.3 MB baseline
  while VmSize grows by the full mapping.
- calloc at huge size is NOT lazy in this core: calloc
  (256 MiB) took 46.7 ms because alloc_locked memsets the
  whole usable extent, faulting every page in at calloc
  time (a same-size malloc+touch pair costs ~36 ms; the
  zero scan-back took 19.2 ms). The bytes are proven
  zero. This is a real posture difference from glibc,
  whose mmap'd calloc returns untouched zero pages.
Failure behaviour (audit above; every case PASS, the
sequence case PASS in both of its runs):
- Arithmetic refusals are clean NULLs before any mapping
  is attempted: malloc(SIZE_MAX), malloc(SIZE_MAX-4095),
  malloc(SIZE_MAX-4096), calloc(2^32 x 2^32) and calloc
  (SIZE_MAX x 2) all return NULL, and the allocator is
  immediately proven live (small + 256 MiB allocations).
- Beyond-VA: malloc(2^48) returns clean NULL from both
  MimirAlloc and glibc; no crash, allocator live after.
- Overcommit: malloc(32 GiB) and malloc(64 GiB) untouched
  SUCCEED under this host's heuristic posture (CommitLimit
  ~186 GiB), RSS growth 118784 B / 4096 B (lazy commitment
  confirmed), and free restores VmSize exactly (delta 0).
  Nothing was touched at these sizes, by design.
- realloc failure is non-destructive: realloc of a
  patterned 256 MiB block to SIZE_MAX returns NULL with
  the pattern byte-intact and bytes_mapped unchanged;
  a following realloc-grow to 1 GiB succeeds and
  preserves the pattern.
- Partial-failure sequence: with 2 GiB live, a SIZE_MAX
  attempt fails cleanly BETWEEN two successful 2 GiB
  allocations; a small allocation succeeds immediately
  after the failure. After all frees, the only residue
  is 4096 B - one retained EMPTY small-path span created
  by the sequence's own small allocation (by-design
  bounded retention, G-007) - released by one trim call,
  after which bytes_mapped and the /proc/self/maps line
  count return EXACTLY to baseline (26 -> 26). The huge
  path itself retains nothing: every huge free munmaps
  its whole mapping (pair case: two 8 GiB blocks live at
  once, both freed, mapped bytes and maps count exact).
  First-attempt honesty: this case initially reported
  FAIL on the same 4096 B residue before its source was
  identified; the audit was corrected (small path warmed
  and trimmed before the baseline) rather than the
  residue being waved away, and the corrected case shows
  the residue's size, source and release explicitly.
