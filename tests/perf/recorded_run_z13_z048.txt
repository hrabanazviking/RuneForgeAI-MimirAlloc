=== THE IMPLEMENTATION (invariants of record) ===
- A freed LARGE mapping whose length the pool can admit is NOT unmapped. Its descriptor stays in its still-mapped reserve page with the new state MIMIR_STATE_POOLED (3u), unlinked from the live registry and linked on a dedicated pool list through the descriptor's own list_next/list_prev fields. A descriptor is on exactly one of {live registry, pool, neither} at any instant.
- Bounds: at most 64 pooled mappings AND at most 32 MiB of pooled bytes (MIMIR_POOL_MAX_COUNT / MIMIR_POOL_MAX_BYTES). A retire that would cross either bound - including a single mapping longer than 32 MiB - takes the banked munmap path instead (overflow trigger, per the roadmap). Bounds are checked overflow-free against current pool bytes under the global lock.
- Reuse is EXACT-LENGTH only (size-segregated by construction): alloc_locked computes the exact mapping_length a fresh acquire would return (checked page round-up) and takes a pooled mapping of that length if one exists. A hit is republished through the IDENTICAL publication code as a fresh mapping - descriptor rewritten in full, flags/reserved zeroed, J-002 owner record rewritten, calloc zeroing applied over the whole usable extent (proven on a dirtied pooled mapping by churn case 2: reuse never leans on the fresh-mmap zero property). A pooled mapping that fails a publication check is returned to the pool, never unmapped (from_pool paths in alloc_locked).
- munmap now happens in exactly three places for large mappings: (a) a retire the pool cannot admit, (b) pool_release_all_locked - called by the FULL trim (mimir_core_trim_retained) and the unload drain (mimir_core_drain_retained, hence also at dlclose through the image destructor), which is the roadmap's trim trigger; trim_retained_some stays span-budget only and never touches the pool, (c) the banked alloc-failure releases of FRESH mappings (unchanged). pool_release_all unlinks and accounts each entry BEFORE its munmap, and a failed release re-pools the still-mapped entry - the pool bookkeeping always describes actual memory.
- Accounting (definitions updated by this slice, stated in mimir_core.c at the pool globals and in mimir_core.h): live_mappings/live_bytes count LIVE only (unchanged); the E-005 audit retirement increments when a descriptor leaves LIVE (pooled or unmapped), so published - retired == live mappings at every instant (unchanged identity); Q-RELEASED (bytes_released) counts bytes ACTUALLY returned to the OS - a pooled retire adds nothing, an overflow unmap and every trim/drain pool release add their length at the munmap; Q-MAPPED (mimir_core_mapped_bytes and the J-002 snapshot's bytes_mapped, and the peak sampler) counts live + POOLED + span bytes - a pooled mapping is still mapped; Q-RETAINED/bytes_cached keep their span-only definitions; the pool's own occupancy is queryable (mimir_core_pool_count/_bytes, INC-E001-07 shape (b) internal pair, test header tests/core/mimir_pool_z048.h). nm -D on the built libmimiralloc.so still shows exactly the 4 public symbols.
- The J-006 metadata walk now also walks the pool: every entry must be POOLED, self-based, zero-fielded as publication wrote it, links consistent, and walked count/bytes equal to the pool counters (0 violations in every churn case below).
- Retention honesty (the price, named by Z-046): after frees, up to 32 MiB / 64 VMAs stay mapped until reuse, trim or drain. That is the deliberate trade this slice buys speed with; RSS after a large free no longer returns to the pre-allocation figure until a trim - Z-050 owns the pressure-driven trim policy that spends this bound down under memory pressure.
